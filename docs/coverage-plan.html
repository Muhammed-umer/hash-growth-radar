Covering every video under a topic

What YouTube's free API gives us, what it does not, and the scheduled jobs that together find every reachable video under our 6 topics and every new comment under those videos. Every API fact below was read from Google's reference pages on 17 September 2026. Built on 17 September 2026: the jobs below are the routes under src/app/api/cron/, the logic is in src/lib/youtube/, the tables in supabase/migrations/0003_watchlist.sql, the schedule in 0002_cron.sql. Not built: job 5 (push notifications) and fetching extra replies with comments.list. Job 4 catches new uploads of followed channels within a day.

1 · The allowance: two separate pots

Google's words: "Projects that enable the YouTube Data API have a default quota allocation of 100 search.list calls, 100 videos.insert calls, and 10,000 units per day combined for all other endpoints." And: "The search.list and videos.insert methods have their own quota buckets. Each of these methods has a default daily limit of 100 per day."

100 searchesper day. One page of search.list = 1 search. Never touches the units.
10,000 unitsper day for everything else. Every call we use costs 1 unit.
Midnight Pacificboth pots reset. 12:30 PM IST in US summer, 1:30 PM IST in US winter.
What this means for the design. Searching is scarce (100 a day). Reading is cheap (10,000 a day). So the plan uses searches only to discover videos and does all the repeated work, checking and reading, with 1-unit calls.

Source: developers.google.com/youtube/v3/getting-started and /determine_quota_cost, read 17 Sep 2026.

2 · The calls, exactly

CallCostPer pageWhat it gives us, and the parameters that matter
search.list 1 search 0 to 50
default 5		 Videos matching a phrase (q). Each result carries the video id, title, channel id and publish time. Parameters we send: part=snippet, type=video, q, maxResults=50; order = date, relevance (Google's default) or viewCount (Google also accepts rating, title, videoCount); publishedAfter / publishedBefore (RFC 3339 time); relevanceLanguage (ISO 639-1); pageToken for the next page. Accepted by Google but not used by us: channelId, regionCode, videoDuration (short under 4 min, medium 4 to 20, long over 20). The answer includes nextPageToken when more pages exist.
channels.list 1 unit many ids
comma list		 For a channel id, contentDetails.relatedPlaylists.uploads = "The ID of the playlist that contains the channel's uploaded videos." We read up to 40 pages (2,000 videos) of it per channel.
playlistItems.list 1 unit 0 to 50
default 5		 The videos in a playlist (playlistId), with pageToken for more. For an uploads playlist the newest upload comes first in practice; Google's page does not state the order. This is how we list a channel's uploads without a single search. Private and deleted placeholders are skipped.
videos.list 1 unit up to 50 ids
our batch size		 For a comma list of video ids: statistics.commentCount ("The number of comments for the video"), statistics.viewCount, contentDetails.duration (ISO 8601, e.g. PT4M13S), snippet.liveBroadcastContent (none unless live or upcoming), snippet.channelId, snippet.publishedAt. One unit tells us whether 50 videos got new comments. Google's page gives no id limit; 50 is the batch the code sends. Google also says maxResults is not supported together with id, so we do not send it.
commentThreads.list 1 unit 1 to 100
default 20		 Top-level comments of a video (videoId), order=time (newest first, the default) or relevance, textFormat=plainText, pageToken. Adding part=replies returns a limited set of replies per thread in the same call (Google: "a limited number of replies"). Each comment has its id, text and publishedAt.
comments.list 1 unit up to 100 parentId= a top-level comment id returns all of its replies. Not used yet (planned: only for threads that already scored well).
Push notifications free — Google's hub (pubsubhubbub.appspot.com/subscribe) sends an HTTP notification to a public URL of ours whenever a subscribed channel "uploads a video" or updates a title or description. Topic URL: https://www.youtube.com/feeds/videos.xml?channel_id=CHANNEL_ID. The notification names the video id and channel id. Google's page does not mention a quota cost. Not built.

Error answers we must handle: quotaExceeded (a pot is empty for the day), commentsDisabled (the video has comments off), videoNotFound (removed or private). A malformed request still costs its call.

Sources: developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/search/list, /channels, /playlistItems/list, /videos, /commentThreads/list, /comments, /guides/push_notifications, read 17 Sep 2026.

3 · What YouTube cannot do for us

  1. There is no "list every video about X". Search returns a ranked selection, not a catalogue. Google says of the total: "The value is an approximation and may not represent an exact value." Nobody, including Google's own page, can tell us the true number of videos for a phrase.
  2. One query runs dry at roughly 500 results. After about 10 pages of 50, nextPageToken stops coming, whatever the true count. Google's page states a 500-video cap only for searches inside one channel (channelId with type=video). For keyword searches like ours the cap is not documented, but developers consistently report it, so we design around it.
  3. The same query gives a different set on different days. Ranking moves. A video seen today may not be in tomorrow's result. So a video must be remembered the moment it is seen.
  4. There is no "comments since last time" filter. Comments come newest first; we have to remember the newest one we saw and stop when we reach it.
  5. The 100 searches are the only scarce thing. Everything else in section 2 costs 1 unit out of 10,000.
So "every video" has to mean: every video that any door of the API can reach, found by walking through all of those doors, remembered permanently, and with a measured miss rate rather than an assumption. Section 9 shows the measurement.

4 · The idea in one picture

Four doors into the same table of videos. Search opens the first three; channels open the fourth. Every video that comes through any door is stored once, by its video id. Reading comments happens from the table, never from a search result, so position in a search result is irrelevant: video number 480 is treated exactly like video number 1.

DoorWhat it findsCostsWhen
A · Month sweepEverything a phrase can return for each month of the year, one month at a time, so the 500 cap applies per month instead of per phrase. Run in two orders (most viewed, newest) because each order surfaces videos the other hides.searchesonce, over the first days
B · New since last lookVideos uploaded after the last time we searched this phrase, newest first, up to 5 pages. Anything older left in that window is handed to the month sweep. Almost never returns a repeat.searches4 times a day
C · Relevance netOne plain relevance search per phrase, no date filter. Catches an older video that became popular late.searchesonce a day
D · Channel uploadsThe upload history (up to 2,000 videos) of every channel that gave us an on-topic video, then its new uploads. No search needed.unitsdaily

Door D is what makes coverage grow beyond what search shows. A channel that made one "thyroid diet" video usually made ten more with titles search did not match. Their comments are the same audience.

Nothing about a commenter is stored: no name, no channel id. The commenter's channel id is compared in memory with the video's channel id to drop the creator's own comments, then discarded.

6 · The jobs, one by one

Job 1 · Month sweep (temporary: runs until every phrase and month is done)

Every 2 hours, at :10 UTC, all day. The one-time deep pass through the year for each of the 6 phrases. The daily limit comes from the search count, not the clock.

  1. Pick the next unfinished window. The cursor lives in sweep_units: a window with a saved page first, otherwise the newest pending window. So the sweep walks backwards: September 2026 most viewed and newest (the current month ends at the moment the sweep was seeded), then August, and so on back to January 2026, for every phrase.
  2. Search it, page by page. q=thyroid diet, type=video, publishedAfter=2026-08-01T00:00:00Z, publishedBefore=2026-09-01T00:00:00Z, order=viewCount, maxResults=50, relevanceLanguage from the phrase. Keep sending pageToken until no nextPageToken comes back, at most 10 pages. Each page is 1 search, and the page token is saved after every page.
  3. Store every video and every channel. New video id: insert with the phrase and the door "sweep". Known video id: add the phrase to its list, refresh its last-seen time, and bring it back to active if it was retired. The channel behind a phrase-found video is inserted and followed at once.
  4. Mark the window done, move to the next. At page 10 the window is split instead if it looks capped: a next page still offered, a full last page, or at least 400 videos. Stop for the day when the day's searches reach 64 (about 30 of the 95 stay for jobs 2 and 3), or at the 240-second time budget. Resume on the next run.
  5. Switch off. When every phrase has every month in both orders marked done, the job reports itself finished and does nothing on later runs. It wakes again only if job 2 hands it a window.
Size. 6 phrases × 9 months × 2 orders = 108 phrase-month-orders. Worst case each hits the cap and costs 10 searches: 1,080 searches, about 17 days at 64 a day. Most months of a niche phrase need far fewer pages, so expect it sooner; the YouTube page shows the real progress. When a window does hit the cap, it is split at its exact midpoint and each half is swept, later half first; halves keep splitting down to 2-day windows.

Job 2 · New videos (permanent)

Every 6 hours. Keeps the table current from the search side.

  1. For each phrase: order=date, publishedAfter= the phrase's last "new since" time minus 1 hour of overlap (publish times can lag), maxResults=50. Up to 5 pages; if more remain, the older part of the window becomes a sweep window for job 1.
  2. Store as in job 1, marked search_new. Then set the phrase's last "new since" time to the run's start. If the search failed, the time is not moved, so the next run catches up.
  3. Cost: usually 1 search per phrase per run because only a handful of videos appear in 6 hours. 6 phrases × 4 runs = usually 24 searches a day (up to 5 pages per phrase per run when a lot is new).

The starting point for a phrase is set when job 1 first seeds it (the current month ends at that instant), so nothing is lost while job 1 is still working. If this job ever runs before job 1 has seeded a phrase, it falls back to "30 days ago".

Job 3 · Relevance net (permanent)

Once a day, inside job 2. In whichever job 2 run comes at least 20 hours after the phrase's last relevance search: one plain search, order=relevance, no date filter, one page of 50. 6 searches a day. Stored as search_relevance. Its purpose is the older video that search suddenly ranks high because it went viral this week.

Job 4 · Channel uploads (permanent)

Once a day, 08:30 UTC (just after the quota reset in summer and winter). Turns every on-topic channel into a source that costs units, not searches.

  1. Resolve new channels. Followed channels without an uploads playlist id: channels.list, up to 50 ids per call, part=snippet,contentDetails. Store the playlist id; a channel without one is unfollowed. (A channel becomes followed the moment a phrase finds one of its videos.)
  2. Check every walked channel's newest page. Channels not listed for 20 hours, up to 600 a run: one page of 50 uploads; another page only if every video on it was new (at most 3). New uploads go into the table marked channel. This runs before the history walks, so new uploads are never starved by a backlog. Usually 1 unit per channel per day.
  3. Walk the history once. A followed channel not yet walked, most on-topic channels first: playlistItems.list on its uploads playlist, 50 per page, until the end or 40 pages (2,000 videos). The page position is saved, so a walk that runs out of time or budget continues next run. At most 2,000 units a run for walks.
  4. Unfollow quietly (done by job 7). A channel first seen over 120 days ago with no on-topic upload in the last 120 days stops being listed daily (its videos stay). It is followed again the moment a phrase finds a video of it.
Why this becomes the main engine. Search can only show what it ranks. A channel's uploads playlist shows what the channel has, every time. Estimate: if a few hundred channels sit behind our 6 phrases, their new uploads arrive here within a day for a few hundred units, and the searches in jobs 2 and 3 become the safety net rather than the source. Uploads by channels we have never met still depend on search.

Job 5 · Push receiver (NOT BUILT, planned)

Not built. The design, for later: for followed channels we also subscribe to Google's free push feed. A public URL of ours answers the hub's verification and then receives a small XML notice with the video id whenever the channel uploads. The video goes straight into the table, marked for its first comment read a few hours later. Subscriptions expire, so a daily renewal cron re-subscribes channels whose lease ends within 3 days (the lease length is not stated on Google's page; developers report up to 10 days, so 3 days of margin is safe). If a push is missed, job 4 catches the upload the next day; push only makes it faster.

Job 6 · Comment reader (permanent)

Every 2 hours. Reads new comments from the table of videos. Never looks at a search result.

  1. Ask which videos changed. Every active video whose next check time has passed: videos.list with 50 ids per call, part=statistics,contentDetails,snippet. Compare commentCount with the stored one. Also refresh duration (Short or not), live status, and mark gone if an id is missing from the answer. 3,000 videos = 60 units. At most 1,000 due videos per run.
  2. Decide which to read. Read a video when its count changed (up or down), or it was published in the last 7 days (counts lag on fresh videos, so read regardless), or it has not been read for 7 days. Shorts: only weekly.
  3. Read to the cursor. commentThreads.list, videoId, order=time, part=snippet,replies, textFormat=plainText, maxResults=100. Page newest first until a comment older than the stored "newest comment seen" appears, then stop. Store the new "newest comment seen". A quiet video costs 1 unit and returns nothing new. Comments dated before 1 January 2026 are ignored.
  4. Big videos are read in slices. At most 10 pages per video per run; a first read of an old video may take up to 30 pages, spread over runs with a resume token. Order: unfinished reads first, then videos whose count moved, then the rest, busiest (most comments) first. Per run at most 300 videos and 1,500 pages; the reader stops for the day once the day's units reach 7,000, so jobs 4 and 8 keep at least 2,000.
  5. Set the next check time. 2 hours if the video is under 7 days old or its count moved; otherwise 24 hours, or 7 days once the count has not moved for 60 days. An unfinished read continues next run; a live broadcast is checked again in 6 hours. A video at least 180 days old whose count has not moved for 180 days becomes retired: not checked again unless a phrase or channel finds it afresh.
  6. Hand over, then save the cursors. Kept comments go through the keyword filter, the AI form, scoring and the Today list. The videos' cursors are saved only after the comments are stored, so a failure can never skip comments. A video selected but not read keeps its old comment count, so the change is seen again next run.

Job 7 · Cleanup (permanent)

Once a day, 03:00 UTC. Deletes stored comments not returned by YouTube for 30 days (Developer Policies III.E.4.d: data may be stored temporarily "not longer than 30 calendar days", then deleted or refreshed). Active video rows are refreshed by job 6. Removed or private (gone) videos not seen for 30 days are deleted; retired and comments-off videos keep only their id after 30 days (title and counts cleared), so they are not re-added as "missing". Unfollowed channels not seen for 30 days are deleted; channels with no on-topic upload for 120 days are unfollowed. Runs older than 60 days are deleted.

Job 8 · Coverage check (permanent)

Once a week, Mondays 09:00 UTC. Picks 20 followed channels whose history walk is done, at random, lists their uploads (up to 40 pages each) and counts videos that are on the channel but not in our table. Missing ÷ listed is the miss rate, shown on the YouTube page, and the missing videos are added on the spot. It is the only honest number for "did we cover everything", and it should read zero for followed channels.

9 · How we know it works

  • Miss rate on followed channels (job 8): should be 0. Anything above 0 is a bug in job 4.
  • Manual spot check once a week: open YouTube, search one phrase with "newest first", take the first 30 videos, check each id is in the table.
  • Both pots on the YouTube page, live from the ledger, plus the Google Cloud console quota graph: searches ≤ 100, units ≤ 10,000 every day.
  • Sweep progress on the YouTube page: sweep windows done out of the total (108 at the start, more after splits), and videos per door.
  • Per-video yield: each video stores how many of its comments passed the filter (refreshed daily). It is not shown on a page yet; it is the input for choosing the next round of phrases.
The honest ceiling. YouTube never publishes the full list of videos for a phrase. This plan walks every door the API has, remembers everything it sees, follows every channel it meets, and measures its own misses. That is the most "every video" the free API allows, and it is far past the 5-videos-per-search the tool did before 17 September 2026.
